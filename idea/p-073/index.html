Who has the pain

US home cooks who save recipes from social video, baited by 'free' apps that need a $40/yr trial

US users who save recipes from TikTok/Instagram/websites feel baited by recipe-import apps: 'free' apps that open with a 7-day trial and $40/yr, 5 imports a week, charges after cancelling, imports that miss ingredients, and paid sync that only works on one platform. Many say they'd pay once, not yearly. Second pass (24 Oct): RecipeBox and Pepper moved saved libraries behind subscriptions, Recipe Keeper charges each spouse separately, and NYT Cooking added AI recipes.

Gap: An honest, cheap importer with no trial trap and a one-time or low annual price. Paprika ($4.99 one-time) and Plan to Eat already cover most of it; the remaining gap is reel import done well, which is a distribution game ReciMe is winning.

Price, MVP and kill question
Price (USD estimate, from the brief)
$14.99/year or $19.99 one-time
MVP
A share-sheet target: share a reel or URL, the app pulls caption, transcript and page schema and shows ingredients and steps in 3 seconds. Tap 'fix' on any line. One list of saved recipes.
What would kill it
Per-import AI/transcript cost above ~$0.02 at $15/yr; ReciMe dropping its 5-per-week cap; users really wanting one-time (Paprika exists); no TikTok distribution.
